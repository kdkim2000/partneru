## Slide 8/36 - Learn, practice, assess, apply

Badges are designed to validate practical capability, not simply course completion.

A useful way to understand badge progression is:

1. Learn ✓ (clicked to complete)
2. Practice ✓ (clicked to complete)
3. Assess or validate ✓ (clicked to complete)
4. Badge recognition ✓ (clicked to complete)

Here's what each stage means.

**Learn**
You complete course content that builds the knowledge and judgment needed for the pathway.

**Practice**
You apply the concepts through exercises, examples, knowledge checks, or other role-relevant tasks.

**Assess or validate**
You complete the assessment or other approved validation requirement defined for the pathway.

**Badge recognition**
A badge signals demonstrated capability under the current pathway requirements.

Live learning opportunities are separate from this sequence. Where available, access may depend on eligibility, invitation, capacity, or other current program criteria.

Badges signal applied readiness, not just participation. Exact badge names and award rules should be confirmed in current PartnerU guidance.

## Slide 18/36 - Deployment, technical, and cyber pathways

Five practitioner pathways focus on deployment, technical judgment, implementation, or cyber workflows:

**ChatGPT Deployment Practitioner**
Plans and supports ChatGPT rollout, activation, adoption, measurement, and organizational scale.

**OpenAI Technical Practitioner**
Builds cross-product technical judgment across solution patterns, architecture, security, governance, data, evaluations, and observability.

**OpenAI Cyber Deployment Practitioner**
A focused deep dive that equips partners to evaluate and safely implement OpenAI-powered cybersecurity solutions through Codex Security workflows, bounded pilots, governance, evidence, success measures, and executive-ready recommendations.

Choose the path that matches the solution area and depth of work you are responsible for.

## Slide 23/36 - What badges demonstrate

Badges represent applied capability rather than simple participation. That distinction matters.

A course completion shows that a learner completed learning content. A badge goes further. It indicates that the learner has demonstrated readiness in a defined area, such as selling, deploying, implementing, or supporting a specific OpenAI solution path.

A badge may demonstrate that a learner can:

- Understand the relevant solution area.
- Apply the right concepts in realistic contexts.
- Make role-appropriate decisions.
- Recognize fit, readiness, risks, or next steps.
- Connect OpenAI capabilities to customer outcomes.

Badges help create a clearer signal of capability across partner roles. They are designed to support confidence: confidence for the learner, confidence for partner teams, and confidence that learners are progressing through the program in a meaningful way.

## Slide 24/36 - Why badges matter

Badges matter because they provide a program-defined signal of demonstrated capability. They can help:

- Demonstrate capability
- Support professional development
- Guide learning progression
- Clarify the pathway or solution area in which capability was validated.

Badges do not automatically guarantee access to live experiences or other entitlements. Those opportunities may depend on invitation, eligibility, program availability, or partner-specific guidance.

Badges are milestones that help learners understand what they have demonstrated and what capability they may want to build next.

Badges mark your progress through PartnerU and can help you choose the capability or pathway you want to build next.

## Slide 25/36 - Assessments and validation

**Assessments**
Check whether you can apply the knowledge and judgment developed through a practitioner pathway. They may ask you to demonstrate role-appropriate understanding, decision-making, or practical capability.

**Validation**
The broader process used to confirm that you have met the requirements for a badge. Depending on current program rules, validation may involve a pathway assessment or another approved activity.

Practice activities help you prepare, but completing a practice activity or course does not by itself meet the requirements for a badge unless current program guidance says so.

Check PartnerU or the Partner Portal for the current badge name, required learning, assessment or validation method, and award criteria.
