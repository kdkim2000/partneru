**Q1. What does Stage 1 mean for an early AI opportunity?**

- The customer has expressed AI interest but no workflow or owner is visible
- ROI has been proven and commercial terms are committed
- **Enough evidence supports deeper joint work and a clear next step, even though the solution, ROI, architecture, and commercial details remain open** ✓ Correct
- Stage 1 is production approval with the final solution, architecture, commercial terms, and implementation plan agreed

*Explanation: Stage 1 means the customer problem, workflow, stakeholders, reason to explore AI, and next step are clear enough for deeper opportunity development. It does not require a finalized solution, proven ROI, known architecture, or committed deal. (Source: Recognizing Strong AI Opportunities)*

**Q2. Which customer behavior is the strongest evidence of commitment to an early opportunity?**

*Explanation: Customer commitment is demonstrated through a meaningful next step, stakeholder participation, and willingness to provide workflow or process detail. A scheduled review with a clear purpose is stronger evidence than positive sentiment alone. (Source: Recognizing Strong AI Opportunities)*

**Q5. Analysts perform document review each day, while an operations leader is accountable for turnaround time and quality. Who is the outcome owner?**

*Explanation: An outcome owner is accountable for improving the workflow or business result and may differ from the people doing the work. Affected users reveal day-to-day friction, while the owner helps validate priority, success, and the next step. (Source: Discovery That Drives Qualification)*

**Q6. A sales operations manager owns the renewal-preparation workflow, team leaders will join a review, and customer information is subject to internal approval processes. Which discovery response is strongest?**

**Q7. Which use-case statement contains the elements needed for a clear, responsible workflow improvement?**

*Explanation: A strong use case identifies the workflow step, user, work object, AI-supported output, human role, and desired outcome. This makes the work change concrete and keeps review or decision responsibility visible. (Source: Designing High-Impact Use Cases)*

**Q8. A claims team wants a narrow first validation that produces an observable output reviewers can check. Which candidate should it start with?**

*Explanation: Extraction offers a clear workflow step and a concrete output that reviewers can compare with source documents. **Q9. Which set provides the most useful success signals for AI-assisted retrieval of approved support policy guidance?**

**Q10. Which sequence best organizes an evidence-based value story?**

*Explanation: The value-building sequence starts with the customer's current work and moves progressively toward the improvement, measurable outcome, business reason, and evidence needed. **Q11. A customer confirms that analysts spend two to three hours per case, but has not decided whether saved time would reduce cost, increase throughput, or improve quality. How should the seller characterize this?**

*Explanation: Customer-confirmed review time is evidence about the current workflow. The way saved capacity would translate into cost, throughput, quality, or another outcome remains an assumption until the customer validates it. (Source: Building the Business Value)*
