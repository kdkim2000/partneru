## Slide 5/13 - Scenario context

You are supporting a ChatGPT Enterprise deployment for Kestrel Manufacturing, a fictional global manufacturing and industrial services company operating across North America and Europe.

Kestrel has approximately 18,000 employees, operations across 12 countries, multiple business units, and corporate, operational, and customer-facing functions.

Its immediate challenge is deciding how to launch with a focused first user group, a clear workflow-entry point, and enough readiness, governance, enablement, and measurement structure to support responsible expansion.

The primary activation workflow is internal knowledge search and procedure summarization for operations teams.

Other workflow ideas may come up, including HR policy summarization and customer-facing response drafting.

Treat these as secondary pressure points unless Evan gives you clear evidence that one should become the first activation priority.

## Slide 8/13 - Activity 2: Identify the readiness or governance gap that matters most

Evan is now using the operations workflow as the working starting point. Before launch, he wants to know whether Kestrel is ready to support the workflow responsibly.

In this conversation, Evan will bring in two stakeholder concerns:

- **Dana Reyes**, Director of Enterprise Technology, is pressing on workspace readiness, ownership, approved knowledge sources, and launch dependencies.
- **Robert King**, Chief Risk Officer, is pressing on governance, data-handling boundaries, review expectations, feedback routes, issue escalation, and unresolved risk ownership.

Dana also reports that one operations team wants to publish a Workspace Agent for this workflow.

The proposal would use an App to retrieve approved SOPs and may post draft summaries to a shared operations channel, but Kestrel has not agreed who can use the Agent, whether the App should use end-user accounts or a shared, agent-owned connection, which read or write actions are permitted, when approval is required, who reviews outputs, or how unexpected actions and access issues are escalated.

Dana and Robert are not separate AI characters. You will still speak only with Evan. Treat their concerns as pressure points that help you decide which readiness or governance gap matters most. Your goal is not to resolve every dependency. Your goal is to identify the gap that most affects whether the first workflow can launch responsibly and recommend a practical next step.

In the conversation, focus on:

- What must be owned, validated, reviewed, or escalated before launch.
- What can be monitored during rollout.
- Who should own the next step.

A strong response should help Evan decide whether the first activation can proceed, should narrow, or needs a specific readiness action before launch.

## Slide 11/13 - Scenario: Kestrel ChatGPT Deployment: Adoption Support and Conditions for Scale (Role-play with Evan Brooks, COO)

For support, we'll set up a small champion group among the ops managers who can answer day-to-day questions, plus a weekly office hour during the first few weeks and a shared channel where anyone can flag confusing outputs, source gaps, or issues. That channel doubles as our feedback loop so we catch problems and update the source guidance quickly instead of letting small issues pile up."

I'd also want confirmation that the source guidance has stabilized, that the Workspace Agent's access and review controls are working as intended with no unresolved escalations, and that the ops managers feel confident reviewing outputs rather than rubber-stamping them. If we see those signals after a few weeks, that's real evidence of adoption and readiness, not just people logging in."

## Slide 12/13 - Debrief: Key deployment pattern

In this scenario, the strongest recommendation is not the broadest rollout plan. It is the clearest next deployment decision.

Use this pattern in ChatGPT deployment conversations:

Deployment challenge → first workflow-entry point → first user group → readiness or governance gap → adoption support → success signals → conditions for scale → practical next step

When a customer raises new concerns, do not treat each concern as a separate workstream. Use it to refine the same deployment decision.

A focused deployment recommendation should show:

- What workflow ChatGPT should support first.
- Why that workflow is the right starting point.
- What must be owned, validated, reviewed, or escalated.
- How users will be supported in daily work.
- What evidence will show whether the activation is useful and responsible.
- What must be true before the customer expands.

That keeps the conversation grounded in Deploying ChatGPT: planning, readiness, activation, enablement, measurement, governance, and scale.
