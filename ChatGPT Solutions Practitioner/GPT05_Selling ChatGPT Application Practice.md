## Slide 2/11 - Introduction

Customers rarely arrive with a perfectly framed ChatGPT opportunity. They may say they want to "use AI," "improve productivity," or "make teams more consistent." Those are useful signals, but they are not enough to recommend a solution path or move an opportunity forward.

In this scenario-based course, you will practice a realistic ChatGPT customer conversation. The goal is to apply solution framing, workflow prioritization, business positioning, and readiness judgment in a connected customer interaction.

This is a practice course, not a scored assessment. Focus on building judgment you can use in a real customer conversation.

## Slide 3/11 - How the AI role-play works

You'll work through three short role-play conversations with Maya Chen from Harbor & Finch.

Each conversation uses the same customer context, but gives you a new chance to practice a different part of the customer discussion: first clarifying the need, then shaping the opportunity, and finally validating readiness and recommending a practical next step.

Maya will respond based on what you ask. Treat the conversation like a real customer discussion: ask focused questions, listen for evidence, and avoid jumping straight into a product pitch.

Your goal is to move from workflow pain, to solution fit, to value, to a practical next step.

## Slide 4/11 - Scenario setup

**Harbor & Finch scenario setup**

You are speaking with Maya Chen, VP of Client Operations at Harbor & Finch, a fictional mid-sized professional services firm.

Maya wants her client-facing teams to prepare faster, produce more consistent client materials, and reduce missed details before important client conversations. Harbor & Finch has heard about ChatGPT Chat, ChatGPT Work, Codex, and API-enabled solutions, but Maya is not sure which experience—or combination—fits the business problem.

Several opportunities may come up:

- Client briefing preparation for account teams.
- Proposal drafting support for business development.
- Internal knowledge search across project notes and templates.
- Meeting follow-up summaries for client-facing teams.

Maya cares about speed, consistency, client-facing quality, safe use of client information, output review, access boundaries, IT/security involvement, and whether the workflow can be validated before broader use.

Your job is not to sell every possible use case. Your job is to clarify the workflow, test the solution path, recommend the strongest starting point, and identify what should happen next.

## Slide 5/11 - Determine whether ChatGPT is the best fit

In Activity 1, your goal is to determine which product experience best fits the workflow.

Do not pitch ChatGPT too early. First, clarify: who performs the work; which workflow is creating friction; what inputs the team uses; what output the team needs; where the work is slow, inconsistent, or risky; whether the work calls for collaborative use of ChatGPT Chat, delegated knowledge work in ChatGPT Work, software engineering in Codex, an embedded or custom API-enabled experience, or a combination; what still needs to be validated before a full recommendation.

A strong first conversation should move beyond "AI productivity" and toward a specific workflow-based opportunity.

## Slide 6/11 - Scenario (Role-play 1: Discovery)

**Scenario:** Discovery for AI-Powered Client Operations at Harbor & Finch. A partner seller meets with Maya Chen, VP of Client Operations at Harbor & Finch, to explore how AI could improve productivity and consistency in client-facing work. Learners practice discovery questioning, initial product-experience judgment across ChatGPT options, and identifying validation gaps before moving toward a full solution.

**Conversation transcript:**

- **Maya Chen:** "Hi there! I'm Maya Chen, VP of Client Operations at Harbor & Finch. I'm exploring how AI could enhance productivity and consistency in our client-facing work. I'm curious about ChatGPT Chat, ChatGPT Work, Codex, and API-enabled solutions. Could you help me understand which might fit our needs best?"
- **Seller (me):** "Thanks for sharing that, Maya. Before I recommend a specific product, I'd like to understand the workflow better. Could you walk me through one of the client-facing tasks that's creating the most friction today—who performs the work, what inputs they use, and what output they need to produce?"
- **Maya Chen:** "Certainly! One of the main pain points is client briefing preparation. Our account teams often handle this, pulling from client notes, prior project records, internal templates, and account history. They need to produce review-ready client briefing notes and meeting preparation summaries, but the process is time-consuming and quality varies."
- **Seller (me):** "That's helpful. Since this involves synthesizing multiple sources—client notes, project records, templates, and account history—into a review-ready output, this sounds like a good fit for delegated knowledge work rather than a simple back-and-forth chat. Before I go further: is any of this client data sensitive, and would account teams expect a human to review the draft briefing before it's used with a client?"
- **Maya Chen:** "Yes, some of the client data can be sensitive, so understanding data boundaries is crucial. Our account teams would definitely expect a human to review the draft briefing before it's used with a client to ensure accuracy and quality."

**Feedback: Good job!**

You effectively advanced discovery toward specific workflows and began forming a product-fit hypothesis.

- Asked targeted questions to clarify users, inputs, and outputs for the workflow.
- Recognized this as delegated knowledge work rather than casual chat usage.
- Probed for data sensitivity and human review expectations early.

Suggestions for improvement:

- Explicitly connect the described workflow to a specific ChatGPT product experience.
- Highlight what further information is needed before giving a full recommendation.
- Confirm business impact metrics like time saved or quality improvements.

## Slide 7/11 - Recommend the priority workflow

In Activity 2, your goal is to recommend ChatGPT Work for the priority workflow and explain the recommendation clearly.

Be ready to explain: which workflow ChatGPT Work should support; what kind of work ChatGPT Work would help employees do; why this workflow matters to Harbor & Finch; how the recommendation connects to speed, consistency, quality, reduced rework, or client-facing readiness; what source materials or business context the workflow depends on; what review, access, or governance expectations should be considered.

The strongest recommendation will not describe ChatGPT Work as a general productivity tool.

It will connect the experience to a specific business workflow, define the review-ready output, explain the context and boundaries the work depends on, and identify the value Harbor & Finch can validate.

Use measured language. For example: "ChatGPT Work could help..." or "This would need to be validated with..." Do not overstate readiness. You will assess readiness more directly in the final activity.

## Slide 8/11 - Scenario (Role-play 2: Recommending ChatGPT Work)

**Scenario:** Recommending ChatGPT Work for Client Briefing Preparation. In this continuation scenario, a partner seller refines their recommendation of ChatGPT Work for client briefing preparation with a VP of Client Operations. Learners practice turning workflow fit into a specific, value-led, and trust-aware recommendation that focuses on delegated work, review-ready outputs, and responsible use of client information.

**Conversation transcript:**

- **Maya Chen:** "In our last discussion, we identified client briefing preparation as a promising starting point for using ChatGPT Work. Could you clarify which specific parts of this workflow ChatGPT Work would handle, and what the review-ready output would look like?"
- **Seller (me):** "ChatGPT Work could help account teams pull together the first draft of a client briefing—synthesizing client notes, prior project records, internal templates, and account history into a structured, review-ready briefing note. The output would be a draft summary the account team reviews and finalizes before any client conversation, not a final client-facing document on its own. This should help with speed and consistency, since today the quality varies person to person. Before we move further, we'd need to validate exactly which systems hold that account history and client notes, and confirm with IT/security what access and data boundaries apply given the sensitivity you mentioned."
- **Maya Chen:** "That sounds like a good start. How would synthesizing these elements make the preparation process faster and more consistent for our account teams? Also, how do we ensure the accuracy of the synthesized information before it's used in client-facing materials?"
- **Seller (me):** "Speed and consistency improve because analysts would no longer manually re-gather and reformat the same information from four separate sources every time; the draft would already be pulled together in a standard structure, so account teams only spend their time reviewing and refining rather than assembling from scratch. On accuracy: nothing would go to a client without a human review step first, and we'd want to define what the reviewer specifically checks—source accuracy, tone, and any sensitive details—so the review is fast but still catches errors before client use."
- **Maya Chen:** "Thank you for clarifying. It makes sense that automating the initial gathering and structuring would save time and ensure consistency. I appreciate the emphasis on a defined human review process to ensure accuracy and catch any errors. This approach seems well thought out."

**Feedback: Good job!**

You met the scenario goals by giving a clear, value-led and trust-aware recommendation.

- Kept the discussion tightly focused on the client briefing workflow.
- Clearly articulated business value around speed, consistency, and reduced rework.
- Explicitly emphasized human review and validation to maintain trust and accuracy.

Suggestions for improvement:

- Describe more concretely what the review-ready brief would look like.
- Clarify which specific sources would be in scope for synthesis.

## Slide 9/11 - Validate readiness and confirm the next step

In Activity 3, your goal is to assess readiness, surface gaps, and recommend the next step the evidence supports.

Assume the opportunity now points toward ChatGPT Work for client briefing preparation. That may be a strong recommendation, but a strong recommendation does not automatically mean the opportunity is ready for rollout or deployment handoff.

Be ready to explain: whether the priority workflow is specific enough; which source materials or business context the workflow depends on; whether access, permissions, and review expectations are clear; whether IT/security or other stakeholders need to be involved; whether the required ChatGPT Work access and administrator enablement are available; what evidence would show the workflow is useful; what evidence would show the workflow is being used responsibly; whether the next step should be more discovery, workflow validation, stakeholder alignment, or handoff.

A strong final recommendation should include: the recommended ChatGPT experience; the priority workflow; the AI-supported work pattern; the business value; the trust or review consideration; the readiness gap or open question; the recommended next action.

Your goal is not to make the opportunity sound more advanced than it is. Your goal is to recommend the next step that the evidence supports.

## Slide 10/11 - Scenario (Role-play 3: Validating Readiness)

**Scenario:** Validating Readiness for ChatGPT Work at Harbor & Finch. In the final part of an ongoing conversation, a partner seller and a client executive discuss whether a ChatGPT Work opportunity for client briefing preparation is ready to advance. Learners practice assessing readiness, surfacing gaps, and recommending a responsible, evidence-based next step without overstating progress.

**Conversation transcript:**

- **Maya Chen:** "Given our previous discussions, I'm curious about how ready we are to move forward with ChatGPT Work for client briefing preparation. Are there specific areas we need to validate or address before considering deployment?"
- **Seller (me):** "To be honest, we're not fully ready for deployment yet, and I want to be upfront about the gaps rather than overstate where we are. The workflow and value are clear: client briefing preparation, drafted by ChatGPT Work, reviewed by account teams before client use, aimed at faster and more consistent briefs. But we still have open questions—we haven't confirmed which systems hold the account history and client notes, what access and data boundaries IT/security will require given the sensitivity, or whether ChatGPT Work is currently enabled and available for the target users. My recommendation is not to jump to deployment planning yet. Instead, the next step should be a focused workflow validation session with account team leads, revenue operations, and IT/security to confirm source access, review expectations, and capability availability. Once those are resolved, we'd be in a strong position for structured handoff."
- **Maya Chen:** "I appreciate your transparency about the readiness gaps. It sounds like a focused workflow validation session with the key stakeholders is indeed the right next step. This will help us confirm source access, review expectations, and ensure ChatGPT Work is enabled for the target users. Once these are addressed, we'll be better positioned for a structured handoff."

**Feedback: Good job!**

You accurately assessed readiness and recommended a realistic, evidence-based next step.

- Clearly articulated the value and structure of the proposed client briefing workflow.
- Openly acknowledged key uncertainties around systems, access, and security requirements.
- Proposed a concrete stakeholder session that aligns with how the firm actually operates.

Suggestions for improvement:

- Highlight business impact metrics more explicitly to strengthen the case for the next step.
- Clarify which stakeholder should own driving the workflow validation session forward.
