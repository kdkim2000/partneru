**Q1.** A customer says, 'We need an AI assistant for sales.' Which question best applies to the course's primary-output test?

*Explanation: The primary-output test asks what the AI should produce for the business user. It turns a vague experience label into a concrete view of the user, workflow, information context, and business output. (Source: ChatGPT Solution Framing & Fit)*

**Q2.** A bank wants an AI research experience embedded directly in its advisor portal from the start. Which solution path does the course indicate is more appropriate?

- **API, because the AI experience must live inside an existing application** ✓ Correct
- ChatGPT, because every knowledge workflow should begin in a workspace
- No solution path can be considered until a complete deployment plan exists
- Codex, because the users work at a bank

*Explanation: The API path is more appropriate when the AI experience must be embedded in a product, portal, application, or business system. ChatGPT may be the first path when business users can validate the workflow directly in a governed workspace. (Source: ChatGPT Solution Framing & Fit)*

**Q3.** Which statement best follows the course's customer-problem framing structure?

- Launch a general assistant for quarterly reviews, let account managers experiment, and use adoption feedback to define the workflow, review process, and expected output later
- The customer wants ChatGPT to improve productivity across sales

*Explanation: The problem frame identifies the affected role, trigger, specific pain, current workaround, business consequence, and desired better state. The account-review statement makes each of those elements visible instead of leading with a product or feature. (Source: ChatGPT Solution Framing & Fit)*

**Q4.** What should a complete ChatGPT fit-check judgment establish?

**Q5.** A sales team asks whether ChatGPT can write follow-up emails. Which reframing moves the conversation from a task to a workflow?

- Treat every email as a separate one-time use case
- Ask users to draft one sample email without examining the surrounding work
- Compare email-writing and summarization features, then ask the team which capability seems most useful after a meeting
- **Map follow-up from call-note review through commitments, drafting, internal next steps, and preparation for the next conversation** ✓ Correct

*Explanation: A task is a single action, while a workflow is a repeated sequence with a clear user, inputs, steps, output, and business purpose. Post-meeting follow-up includes the work around the email and therefore creates a more useful workflow opportunity. (Source: Designing ChatGPT Workflows)*

**Q6.** A customer proposes 'summarize support tickets.' Which discovery approach best maps the workflow before recommending ChatGPT's role?

- List summarization features, compare prompt styles, and ask the customer which capability seems most relevant to support tickets
- **Identify the user, work object, trigger, inputs, steps, decisions, output, and business purpose** ✓ Correct
- Define the output but leave the user, trigger, inputs, and business purpose for deployment
- Ask only how many tickets the team receives

*Explanation: The simple workflow map covers eight elements: user, work object, trigger, inputs, steps, decisions or actions, output, and business purpose. That structure keeps discovery grounded in how work happens rather than in a feature list. (Source: Designing ChatGPT Workflows)*

**Q7.** Which statement is the strongest prompt-to-output pattern for renewal brief preparation?

- Users can experiment with prompts and decide later what output they need

*Explanation: A practical pattern names the inputs, the work ChatGPT supports, the business artifact produced, and the person who reviews or uses it. This makes ChatGPT's role recognizable without overcomplicating the workflow. (Source: Designing ChatGPT Workflows)*

**Q8.** Two workflows appear valuable. One is ambitious but has unclear ownership and source materials; the other has a recurring trigger, recognizable output, strong user pull, and an easy validation path. Which should the seller recommend exploring first?

- The ambitious workflow, because scope creates stronger executive interest
- Both at once, because prioritization should wait until deployment

*Explanation: When two workflows look promising, the course favors the one with the clearest trigger and output, strongest user pull, and easiest validation path. A moderately valuable workflow that is repeatable and reviewable can be a better first recommendation than an ambitious idea with unclear dependencies. (Source: Designing ChatGPT Workflows)*

**Q9.** Which statement connects a business outcome to the ChatGPT mechanism most effectively?

**Q11.** The customer has not provided a baseline or validated financial assumptions. Which value statement is most defensible?

**Q12.** A customer asks whether ChatGPT can be trusted to prepare customer-facing responses. Which seller response best follows the course's trust-aware pattern?

- Design the customer's security architecture, including permissions, data boundaries, monitoring, and review workflows, during the sales call
- Guarantee accuracy because the workflow uses approved sources

*Explanation: The trust-aware pattern is acknowledge, position, and validate. It treats the concern as legitimate, keeps ChatGPT in a responsible supporting role, and names the source, access, data, and review questions that must be confirmed. (Source: Positioning ChatGPT for Business)*

**Q13.** A workflow appears to be a strong ChatGPT fit, but the business outcome owner, source boundaries, and agreed next step are still unclear. What is the correct readiness judgment?

*Explanation: Readiness is not the same as fit. Before handoff, the notes must make the problem, workflow, people, value, next step, source context, boundaries, and review expectations clear enough that another team can continue without guessing. (Source: ChatGPT Opportunity Readiness & Handoff)*

**Q16.** A customer has a promising workflow, but its exact process, input sources, and output expectations are still unclear. Which next-step category best matches the evidence?

**Q17.** A customer is considering four AI workflows. Client briefing preparation is recurring, owned by a defined account team, and produces a clear review-ready output. The other ideas have unclear ownership, inputs, or validation paths. Which recommendation is strongest?

*Explanation: The strongest starting workflow has a defined user group, recurring pain, a recognizable output, and a practical validation path. **Q18.** A team needs to combine approved business context, complete several knowledge-work steps, and return a review-ready briefing for human approval. Which experience is the strongest first fit?

- **ChatGPT Work, because the team can delegate a bounded, multi-step outcome using approved context and human review** ✓ Correct
- An API-enabled experience, because multi-source knowledge work should always be embedded inside an existing business application
- Codex, because producing a structured, review-ready business document is primarily a software-engineering workflow
- ChatGPT Chat, because employees must stay closely involved in every drafting and synthesis step throughout the workflow

*Explanation: ChatGPT Work fits delegated, employee-facing knowledge work. The team can define the desired outcome, provide approved context and boundaries, delegate several preparation steps, and review the completed work before it is used. ChatGPT Chat is more appropriate for closely collaborative work, Codex is specialized for software engineering, and an API-enabled path is appropriate when intelligence must be embedded in an application or system. (Source: Selling ChatGPT Application Practice)*

**Q19.** A customer is concerned that a delegated briefing workflow may use sensitive client information or produce inaccurate content. What is the strongest seller-level response?

*Explanation: A seller should surface trust considerations early without promising accuracy or attempting to design the customer's detailed security controls.
