## Slide 2/11 - Introduction

Enterprise Codex evaluations rarely stay focused on one simple question.

A customer may start by asking whether AI can help engineering teams move faster, then quickly raise delivery pressure, governance concerns, executive value questions, and next-step uncertainty.

In this course, you'll practice guiding a focused Codex evaluation conversation by keeping the discussion anchored in the customer's most important software delivery workflow.

## Slide 3/11 - Overview

In this scenario, you are working with Aureon Systems, a large software company trying to improve delivery predictability across distributed engineering teams.

Aureon is exploring AI-assisted engineering workflows as part of an internal initiative called Velocity Horizon.

The company is not only asking whether AI can help developers write code faster. It is trying to understand whether Codex can support a more consistent, reviewable, and measurable software delivery workflow.

The conversation centers on inconsistent pull request, or PR, review and release-readiness practices. These workflow issues are creating delays, rework, and uneven delivery confidence across teams.

Your goal is not to respond to every possible customer concern at once. Your goal is to listen for what matters most in the moment, then use the right information to keep the conversation moving.

In the scenario, you will practice how to:

- Clarify the engineering workflow problem.
- Address one governance concern without overreaching.
- Respond to executive value pressure.
- Recommend a focused next step.

This is guided application practice. Stay at the seller level, keep the conversation simple, and bring in technical specialists when the conversation moves beyond seller-level guidance.

## Slide 4/11 - How the AI role play works

You will complete two short role-play activities with Elena Voss, CTO of Aureon Systems.

In each activity:

- Read the setup first.
- Respond as a partner seller or pre-sales partner.
- Keep your responses concise and customer-focused.
- Do not try to solve every issue in one answer.
- Let Elena's latest concern guide your response.

The AI character will close the conversation when you have addressed the main goal of the activity.

As you respond, stay focused on the customer's workflow. Position Codex around delegated engineering work across the SDLC—not as a generic coding assistant.

When Elena raises concerns about governance, value, or competitor comparison, respond at the seller level and avoid moving into architecture, authentication, permission models, configuration, implementation controls, or ROI guarantees.

## Slide 5/11 - Activity 1: Clarify the workflow problem

You are supporting a Codex evaluation for Aureon Systems. Aureon has approximately 5,200 engineers across North America, Europe, and APAC, with multiple distributed engineering organizations supporting enterprise-scale software delivery.

Over the past 18 months, teams have struggled with inconsistent PR review and release-readiness practices. This has created rework, release delays, coordination overhead, and uneven delivery confidence.

Aureon has launched an internal initiative called Velocity Horizon to modernize SDLC operations, improve engineering consistency, and standardize AI-assisted engineering practices.

In this first activity, you will speak with Elena Voss, Aureon's CTO. Your goal is to clarify the workflow problem before positioning Codex.

Listen for what Elena is actually asking. Ask concise discovery questions. Avoid bringing in governance, executive value, or competitor discussion before those concerns are raised.

A strong response in this activity should help Elena see that the evaluation should focus on the workflow where Aureon needs improvement: PR review and release-readiness.

## Slide 6/11 - Scenario: Clarifying Aureon's PR Review and Release-Readiness Workflow

**Roleplay with:** Elena Voss, Chief Technology Officer, Aureon Systems

**Scenario description:** A partner seller meets with Aureon Systems' CTO, Elena Voss, to clarify where PR review and release-readiness workflows are breaking down across distributed engineering teams. Learners practice focused discovery, tying workflow issues to business impact and Velocity Horizon goals before positioning Codex or discussing broader concerns.

**Conversation transcript:**

> **Elena Voss:** Hello, I'm Elena Voss, CTO of Aureon Systems. We're currently facing challenges with inconsistent PR review and release-readiness practices, leading to rework and delays. I'm looking to understand where our workflow is failing to improve our delivery predictability. Can you help clarify this issue?
>
> **Seller (learner):** Thanks for the context, Elena. Before we talk about solutions, I'd like to understand the workflow better. When PRs stall or releases slip, is it mostly happening at the review stage — reviewers taking too long or feedback being inconsistent — or later, at release-readiness, where teams aren't confident the code is ready to ship?
>
> **Elena Voss:** Great question. The inconsistencies are most visible during the PR review stage, where feedback can be inconsistent and timelines unpredictable. This often leads to rework just before major releases, impacting our confidence in release-readiness and causing delays.
>
> **Seller (learner):** That makes sense — inconsistent review feedback is a common bottleneck. Given Velocity Horizon's goal of more predictable delivery, it sounds like the highest-value place to focus first is standardizing and accelerating that PR review stage, so teams walk into release-readiness with more confidence. Does that match how you'd prioritize it?
>
> **Elena Voss:** Yes, that aligns perfectly with our priorities. Standardizing the PR review process to ensure consistent feedback and timely reviews would greatly enhance our delivery predictability. Thank you for helping clarify this focus.

**Result: Good job!** ✓ You effectively clarified the workflow issue and linked it to delivery confidence concerns.

What went well:

- Quickly focused the discussion on PR review and release-readiness breakdowns. ✓
- Used concise discovery questions to connect workflow friction to predictability and rework. ✓
- Resisted jumping into solutioning or Codex positioning prematurely. ✓

What could be improved:

- Probe more into how different teams' practices vary across locations or squads.
- Ask follow-up questions about specific failure cases to deepen understanding.

## Slide 7/11 - What to carry forward

You've clarified the workflow Elena cares about most: PR review and release-readiness.

Next, Elena will return with new pressure from security, finance, and engineering teams comparing tools. Do not restart discovery from scratch. Use the workflow you clarified as the anchor for your recommendation.

## Slide 8/11 - Activity 2: Recommend a focused Codex evaluation path

Elena has reviewed the initial discussion with two internal stakeholders.

**Cameron Ibarra, VP of Platform Security** — Wants to know whether AI-assisted engineering work can remain reviewable, accountable, and visible as adoption expands.

**Adrian Cole, CFO** — Wants confidence that the evaluation can show measurable operational value within the next two planning cycles.

Elena also mentions that some teams are comparing Codex with other tools based on autocomplete quality and code-generation speed.

Your primary objective is to recommend a focused Codex evaluation path. Do not treat governance, value pressure, and competitor comparison as separate conversations to solve one by one. Treat them as inputs that should shape a practical recommendation.

In this activity, your recommendation should:

- Stay anchored in Aureon's PR review and release-readiness workflow.
- Account for governance concerns without designing the deployment.
- Connect value pressure to measurable workflow outcomes.
- Keep the competitor mention brief and tied to evaluation criteria.
- Identify where technical specialists should support validation.

Keep the seller-level boundaries from the setup in mind: recommend the evaluation path, but do not design the deployment or make unsupported ROI or competitor claims.

## Slide 9/11 - Scenario: Recommending a Focused Codex Evaluation for Aureon Systems

**Roleplay with:** Elena Voss, Chief Technology Officer, Aureon Systems

**Scenario description:** A partner seller meets with Aureon's CTO, Elena Voss, to recommend a focused Codex evaluation path centered on PR review and release-readiness. Learners practice keeping the conversation anchored on the key workflow while addressing governance concerns, value expectations, and light competitor pressure at a seller level.

**Conversation transcript:**

> **Elena Voss:** Hello! I'm looking to discuss how we can tailor a focused evaluation plan for Codex as part of our Velocity Horizon initiative. It's important for us to ensure that this evaluation addresses governance concerns and demonstrates operational value. How do you think we should proceed to meet these needs effectively?
>
> **Seller (learner):** Good place to start. Given what we clarified earlier, I'd recommend evaluating Codex on that same PR review and release-readiness workflow first, rather than a broad rollout. That keeps things focused enough to show real value quickly, and it gives Cameron's team a bounded scope to evaluate review responsibility and visibility on, rather than an open-ended governance conversation.
>
> **Elena Voss:** That sounds like a solid approach. Focusing on the PR review and release-readiness workflow should help us demonstrate measurable improvements quickly, which is crucial for Adrian. It also provides a clear scope for Cameron's team to assess reviewability and visibility. How do you suggest we measure the success of this evaluation?
>
> **Seller (learner):** For Adrian, I'd track a couple of simple signals within one or two planning cycles: review turnaround time and rework rate on the PRs going through this workflow. Those directly reflect the delivery predictability problem you described, and they're easy to report on without waiting for a longer transformation program. On the tool comparisons your teams are running — autocomplete and generation speed are fine inputs, but I'd anchor the evaluation criteria on this workflow outcome rather than isolated feature benchmarks, since that's what actually matters to Velocity Horizon. Once we're aligned on that, I'd bring in our technical specialists to work with Cameron's team on the specific review and visibility requirements for this workflow.
>
> **Elena Voss:** I agree, focusing on review turnaround time and rework rate will give us clear indicators of improvement. Anchoring the evaluation on workflow outcomes rather than feature benchmarks aligns perfectly with our goals for Velocity Horizon. Let's proceed with involving technical specialists to ensure Cameron's governance concerns are thoroughly addressed. Does that sound good to you?

**Result: Good job!** ✓ You effectively anchored the evaluation on Aureon's key workflow and addressed governance and value concerns.

What went well:

- Kept the evaluation tightly focused on the PR review and release-readiness workflow. ✓
- Named clear, measurable workflow signals without overpromising ROI. ✓
- Redirected tool-level comparisons toward workflow outcomes and brought in specialists appropriately. ✓

What could be improved:

- Clarify how long the initial evaluation window should run.
- Explicitly state how governance owners will sign off on the evaluation outcome.
